# Build report — F4b Reference barcode — attempt 1

Summary: `npm run data` now also writes `public/data/colorado/barcode.json`. It is computed by F3's `pipeline/tda_ref.compute_h0` on `grid.f32` as read back from disk, with 4-connectivity and the §6.1 tie-break. It holds 600 bars (599 finite with persistence ≥ 1 m, plus the essential bar) in 33,492 B, and two runs give byte-identical files. The essential bar is born at `meta.min.cell` 133025 (−2.885 m), and the build stops if it isn't. `docs/data.md` has the generated top-10 lakes table and a sourced geography check: Bonneville/Great Salt Lake is yes (rank 3); the Great Divide Basin is yes, but only rank 47 (94 m). F4a check notes 1–3 are fixed, and `grid.f32`, `hillshade.webp` and `ATTRIBUTION.md` are byte-identical to the F4a commit.

Acceptance criteria (self-check):
1. ✅ barcode.json follows §6.5 step 5, is 33,492 B (≤ 600 KB) and is reproducible.
   - Columns are `b, d, bi, di` plus `mi` (mergedInto), with `d = null` for the essential bar. `di = mi = -1` for the essential bar, matching the engine's convention.
   - Metadata includes connectivity, tieBreak, minPersistence, units, rounding, order, count, stats, the essential birth cell, and the grid file's width, height, bytes and SHA-256.
   - Values are **not rounded**. They are the exact float32 heights, written as shortest round-trip doubles, so `b[k] === heights[bi[k]]` in JS. No ≥ 1 m bar can look shorter, and nothing is reordered.
   - Tests check: byte-identical output over two fresh runs on a copy of grid.f32 versus the committed file; every finite bar has b < d and d − b ≥ 1; bi, di and mi are in [0, W·H); b and d equal the grid values; bi, di and mi equal `compute_h0` and the `tda_ref` CLI; counts match.
2. ✅ The essential bar has bi = 133025 = `meta.min.cell` = argmin(grid), b = −2.885450839996338. It is tested, and the build fails if it doesn't hold.
3. ✅ The lakes table in docs/data.md is complete.
   - It lists the top 10 with birth and death elevations, birth, death and merge cells, and lat/lon (cell centres, i = y·W + x, north row first). The essential bar is stated separately.
   - The table was generated by code and pasted unedited. The interpretation prose quotes numbers from that output.
- F4a criteria still hold: pytest passes; sizes are within budget (nothing in public/data/colorado is over 600 KB); ATTRIBUTION.md is unchanged; `meta.bbox` is exact (tested); raw tiles are not touched; barcode.json is not gitignored.

Geography findings (docs/data.md; not for the site without checking):
- Top 10 persistence ranges from 466.2 to 263.6 m.
- **6 of the top 10 are born on the east or north edge of the rectangle** (#1, 2, 4, 8, 9, 10). These are valleys that drain out across an edge and become closed in the grid. This is now documented as a limitation in docs/data.md and in `meta.limitations`.
- #3 (395.8 m, born 1275.7 m at 40.7704 N, 113.6646 W) is 9.5 km from the Great Salt Lake Desert's coordinates. Before it spills (1671.5 m, 37.5704 N, 113.1896 W) it contains the Great Salt Lake's coordinates too, so **the Bonneville expectation is met**.
  - That spill point is the lowest exit inside the rectangle. The real outlet, Red Rock Pass at 42.356 N, is outside the rectangle.
- #5, #6 and #7 are 2-cell pits that are 2.3, 1.0 and 0.7 km from the listed coordinates of the Ray, Bingham Canyon and Sierrita open-pit mines.
  - These are real surface-model depressions, kept by the block minimum. I labelled #5 "probably", because the mine's own cell isn't inside the lake.
- **Great Divide Basin:** lake 2273 (born 1943.8 m at 41.9954 N, 107.2646 W, row 6) contains both the basin's listed coordinates and Wamsutter, WY, before it spills at 2037.8 m. Its persistence is 94.0 m, rank 47 of 599. So it is present but not among the longest-lived, and the north edge cuts it.
- Sources: Wikipedia pages for the Great Salt Lake Desert, Great Salt Lake, Bingham Canyon Mine, Ray Mine, Sierrita mine, Great Divide Basin, Wamsutter, Lake Bonneville and Red Rock Pass. They are linked in docs/data.md and their coordinates are kept in `barcode.LANDMARKS`.
- I did not test the RMNP and Green River headwaters statements against a source. They are recorded only as spec context.

Known limitations:
- meta.json still changes on every run (`generatedAt`, `gitCommit`), as in F4a. barcode.json has no timestamp.
- The landmark sources are Wikipedia coordinate listings, not primary gazetteers: USGS GNIS is a JS app, as the F4a checker also found. The interpretations are worded cautiously, and the edge lakes are left uninterpreted beyond "edge-closed".
- The spec allows rounding to 0.1 m, but I chose exact values. If F6 prefers rounded values, both rounding and the ≥ 1 m threshold check would need revisiting. F6 can compare `b` and `d` with the grid using strict equality.
- `mi` (mergedInto) is an addition to the spec's columns, in line with DECISIONS #31.
